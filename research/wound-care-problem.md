# Wound Care Problem Research

## Overview

Wound care is a significant healthcare challenge in the UK and globally. Wounds can be caused by surgery, injury, diabetes, pressure damage, poor circulation, burns, or long-term health conditions.

Some wounds heal normally, but others become delayed, infected, or chronic. This can lead to pain, reduced mobility, repeated clinical visits, higher healthcare costs, and lower quality of life for patients.

TurmiPatch AI is exploring how artificial intelligence and smart wound monitoring could help improve visibility into wound healing progress and support earlier identification of delayed healing risks.

## UK Wound Care Burden

Research published in BMJ Open estimated that in 2017/2018, the NHS managed approximately 3.8 million patients with a wound. The annual NHS cost of wound management was estimated at £8.3 billion.

This means wound care represents a major pressure on healthcare resources, including nurses, GPs, hospital services, wound dressings, and community care.

An industry summary of the same wound burden research states that 3.8 million wound patients is equivalent to around 7% of the adult population, and that 67% of wound management costs are spent on unhealed wounds.

## Key Problems in Current Wound Care

### 1. Delayed healing

Many wounds do not heal as expected. Delayed wound healing can increase the risk of infection, pain, complications, and repeated treatment.

### 2. Infection risk

Infected wounds can become serious if they are not identified early. Patients may not always know when a wound is becoming worse, especially when monitoring happens at home between clinical appointments.

### 3. High cost to healthcare systems

Wound care requires repeated clinical time, dressing changes, treatment reviews, and sometimes hospital care. The estimated annual NHS wound-care cost of £8.3 billion shows the scale of this burden.

### 4. Limited continuous monitoring

Traditional wound care often depends on periodic checks. This can make it difficult to track daily healing patterns or identify early warning signs between appointments.

### 5. Patient quality of life

Wounds can affect sleep, mobility, confidence, work, independence, and mental wellbeing. Chronic wounds may require long-term care and can be emotionally stressful for patients.

## Why AI Could Help

Artificial intelligence could support future wound care by analysing patterns that may not be easy to observe manually.

Potential AI-supported features could include:

- tracking wound size changes over time
- monitoring redness, swelling, moisture, and pain scores
- identifying delayed healing patterns
- flagging possible infection risks
- generating recovery progress scores
- supporting clinicians with structured patient data
- helping patients understand when to seek medical advice

## TurmiPatch AI Opportunity

TurmiPatch AI aims to explore whether a smart wound patch concept could combine:

- turmeric-inspired natural healing research
- wound recovery monitoring
- AI-based healing progress analysis
- early risk detection
- patient and clinician insights

The goal is not to replace healthcare professionals, but to explore how AI could support safer, more informed, and more personalised wound care.

## Potential Future Impact

If clinically validated in future, AI-supported wound monitoring could potentially help:

- reduce delayed healing complications
- improve early detection of wound deterioration
- reduce unnecessary clinical visits
- support faster intervention when needed
- improve patient confidence and self-monitoring
- reduce pressure on healthcare services

Any claim about reducing wound healing time would require proper clinical research, testing, and regulatory approval. At this stage, TurmiPatch AI is a research and concept-development project.

## Research Notes

Important statistics identified during early research:

- The NHS managed around 3.8 million wound patients in 2017/2018.
